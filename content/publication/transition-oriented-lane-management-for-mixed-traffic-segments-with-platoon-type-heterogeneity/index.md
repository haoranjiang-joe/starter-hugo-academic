title: Transition-oriented lane management for mixed-traffic segments with
  platoon-type heterogeneity
publication_types:
  - "0"
  - "2"
abstract: The gradual transition toward connected and automated mobility leads
  to prolonged coexistence of heterogeneous vehicles—human-driven vehicles
  (HVs), connected HVs (CHVs), automated vehicles (AVs), and connected automated
  vehicles (CAVs)—whose relative penetrations evolve continuously from HV-only
  to CAV-only traffic. This coexistence creates significant challenges for lane
  management. This paper develops a Markov-chain-based analytical framework to
  model expected platoon length and lane capacity, explicitly capturing
  heterogeneous platoon types, including pure CAV platoons and mixed CAV–CHV
  platoons, across multiple lane types. Based on these analytical results, an
  Elastic Dedicated-lane Optimization model is formulated as a mixed-integer
  nonlinear program (MINLP) to determine optimal lane allocations, with a
  tailored heuristic solver developed to efficiently obtain high-quality
  solutions. Simulation and validation results show that heterogeneous platoons
  substantially enhance lane capacity even at moderate CAV penetration. Optimal
  lane allocations dynamically adapt to vehicle composition and penetration
  levels, demonstrating the model’s flexible and transition-oriented strategy
  for mixed-traffic-lane management. Sensitivity analysis indicates that CAV
  penetration, platoon length, and lane number critically influence performance,
  while further tests confirm the robustness of the main findings to variations
  in key behavioral and model parameters. Overall, the proposed framework
  provides an analytical basis for adaptive lane-management decisions and
  supports infrastructure planning under evolving mixed-traffic conditions.
