publication: "*Autonomous Transportation Research*"
abstract: Adaptive cruise control (ACC) and cooperative adaptive cruise control (CACC) systems have been widely adopted to improve driving comfort and reduce driver workload. However, from the perspective of traffic flow theory, conventional ACC/CACC models still suffer from several fundamental limitations, including insufficient string stability, capacity degradation near the critical density, the absence of hysteresis behavior, and limited robustness in mixed traffic environments. To address these limitations, this study proposes a macroscopic–microscopic coupled adaptive cruise control model, termed M²ACC. The proposed model incorporates three key innovations: (1) an asymmetric time-headway strategy that distinguishes between congestion formation and congestion dissipation phases; (2) a macroscopic density feedforward term that adjusts longitudinal acceleration according to the estimated traffic density within a 1.5 km downstream range; and (3) a stochastic perturbation mechanism under low-speed conditions to break the potential synchronized oscillations of homogeneous vehicle platoons. Simulation experiments show that, compared with the conventional ACC model, the proposed M²ACC model substantially reduces the disturbance amplification factor and shortens the congestion dissipation time by 54.0%. Compared with the intelligent driver model (IDM), M²ACC increases bottleneck capacity by 22.8%. In mixed traffic at a density of 20 veh/km/lane, increasing the M²ACC penetration rate from 0% to 90% reduces the coefficient of variation of time headway by 85.06%. Overall, the results demonstrate the potential of M²ACC to improve disturbance attenuation and congestion recovery under the investigated conditions, while the contributions of individual control mechanisms depend on the traffic conditions and evaluation metrics.
